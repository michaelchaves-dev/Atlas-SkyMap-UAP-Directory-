Case File 001 — November 13, 2026

The night this live layer was built for. One timeline, separate events kept separate — check each sighting report from that evening against the same minutes.

  1. 3:36 p.m. ET — FAA identifies radar/communications instability.
  2. ~4:00 p.m. ET — Nationwide departure ground stop issued; aircraft already airborne keep landing.
  3. 5:47 p.m. ET — SpaceX Starship Flight 15 launches from Starbase, Texas (pre-announced). Sunset plume — the "space jellyfish" — is visible across Florida; flight crosses the West Palm Beach area ~5:55 p.m. ET. Booster catch called off, booster splashes down in the Gulf; Ship 42 reaches orbit.
  4. Evening — Separate witness reports describe a compact glowing ball descending at an angle. That signature is not a jellyfish plume (which expands, hangs and fades high up). No location-pinned corroboration for a touchdown has been established in the public record checked so far — it stays in the register as unresolved, not explained away.
  5. 8:19 p.m. ET — Formal national ground-stop lift (FAA Advisory 005); airline-specific stops clear in stages, the last at 1:27 a.m. ET Nov 14 (Chicago O'Hare, American).

Method: every report gets timestamped and pinned, then tested against ADS-B aircraft tracks, CelesTrak satellite positions and NOAA GOES data for those exact minutes. What survives those checks stays flagged unresolved.

How verification works

  1. Timestamp first. An entry with no reliable date, time and place does not enter the register.
  2. Satellite correlation. The time and position are checked against published satellite tracks — most “trains of lights” resolve here.
  3. Aircraft & balloons. Flight activity and known balloon transits are checked next.
  4. Space weather. Solar and geomagnetic conditions from NOAA are noted for light-phenomenon reports.
  5. Honest label. Explained, Mixed evidence, Government released, or Unresolved — and the correlation note says exactly what was checked.
